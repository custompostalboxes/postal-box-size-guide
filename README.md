# Postal Box Size Guide

Royal Mail parcel sizes for UK domestic post depend on the finished package, not just the product inside it. This guide sets out the maximum external dimensions and weight limits for Letter, Large Letter, Small Parcel and Medium Parcel, so UK businesses can choose a postal box that fits the service they intend to use.

## Royal Mail parcel sizes at a glance

| Format | Maximum external dimensions | Maximum weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Letter | 240 × 165 × 5 mm | 100 g |
| Large Letter | 353 × 250 × 25 mm | 750 g |
| Small Parcel | 450 × 350 × 160 mm | 2 kg |
| Medium Parcel | 610 × 460 × 460 mm | 20 kg |

These are maximum packed dimensions. If one relevant measurement is exceeded, the parcel moves into a higher size band.

## Letter

A Letter can measure up to 240 × 165 × 5 mm and weigh up to 100 g. It is included here for context, because most boxed products exceed the 5 mm depth limit and therefore need a larger format.

## Large Letter: the 25 mm external-depth rule

A Large Letter can measure up to 353 × 250 × 25 mm. For standard Royal Mail 1st Class and 2nd Class services, the maximum weight is 750 g. For online consumer Tracked 24 and Tracked 48, Royal Mail currently lists a maximum weight of 1 kg for this format, so always check the exact service and purchase channel before posting.

The 25 mm figure is an external-depth rule. It applies to the finished, closed package, not to the internal depth of an empty box. The board, inserts, folded closures, tape and any protective packing all count towards the finished depth, so a box with a 25 mm internal depth will exceed 25 mm once it is packed. ## Small Parcel

A Small Parcel can measure up to 450 × 350 × 160 mm and weigh up to 2 kg on the standard domestic services covered here. Royal Mail defines the length as the longest side of the parcel, so measure the other dimensions across the remaining sides of the finished package. The extra depth gives much more room for a postal box and protective packing than a Large Letter, but a box that is unnecessarily tall can still push a parcel close to the 160 mm limit. ## Medium Parcel

A Medium Parcel can measure up to 610 × 460 × 460 mm and weigh up to 20 kg on the standard consumer services covered here. At this size, the box also has to support the weight of its contents through handling and delivery, so choose a suitable board and structure as well as the right dimensions. Royal Mail directs parcels in the 20-30 kg range to Parcelforce, and businesses using a negotiated or account service should check that exact specification.

## How to measure and weigh a finished parcel

1. Pack the product exactly as it will be sent, including inserts, tissue, padding, documents and tape.
2. Measure the outside of the closed package, with the longest side recorded as the length.
3. Weigh the complete package, because the box and all packing materials count towards the postal weight.
4. Check the exact Royal Mail service and purchase channel, because weight limits can differ between services.
5. Check the contents as well as the size, because Royal Mail restricts or prohibits certain items.

## Common mistakes that push a parcel into a higher size band

- Measuring the product instead of the posted package.
- Using internal dimensions for a carrier limit, when Royal Mail classifies the finished exterior.
- Treating 750 g as the only Large Letter limit, without checking the service being used.
- Ignoring a bulging lid or closure that increases the finished external depth.
- Assuming that a box name guarantees compliance, when the name only describes its intended use.
- Forgetting the weight of the box and packing materials.

## Plan the box around the finished parcel

Custom box sizing can help keep a parcel in a lower Royal Mail band when the product and the protection it needs already fit within that band, particularly for repeat shipments close to the 25 mm or 160 mm depth limits. It cannot make an inherently larger product qualify for a smaller band, and reducing box size should never compromise the protection the product needs. Start with a measured packed sample, then specify the box around it.
